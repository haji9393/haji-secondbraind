# Haji LinkedIn Writing Style Skill

## Khi nào dùng

Apply Haji's personal LinkedIn writing voice when drafting any post, caption, thought leadership piece, or professional update, in Vietnamese, English, or bilingual format. Trigger whenever the user says "viết post LinkedIn", "draft bài LinkedIn", "viết theo kiểu của tôi", "LinkedIn post", "bài viết", or any request to write or adapt professional content for social/personal brand publishing. Also trigger for event recaps, partnership announcements, campaign case studies, and personal reflection posts. This skill defines Haji's tone, structure, and content philosophy - always read before drafting.

Haji writes primarily for LinkedIn, in a dual Vietnamese/English voice depending on audience.
These posts sit at the intersection of practitioner insight and personal brand: grounded in real work, never abstract.

---

## HARD FORMATTING RULES (non-negotiable)

These override everything else:

**BANNED characters and patterns:**
- The em dash character " - " is STRICTLY FORBIDDEN anywhere in the post body. Replace with: a colon ":", a period, a line break, or rewrite the sentence. This is the #1 AI-sounding marker to eliminate.
- No en dash "–" in prose either. Use a hyphen "-" only when grammatically necessary (compound adjectives).
- No ellipsis "..." used dramatically. If needed, keep it conversational.
- No H2/H3 headers (##, ###) inside the post body.

**REQUIRED structure: Luận điểm + Bullet points**
Every post must have a clear thesis (luận điểm) followed by bullet points that support or expand it. This is the primary structural pattern:
- Hook/opening = sets the luận điểm (the one central argument or observation)
- Body = bullet points (3-6 bullets) each carrying one concrete idea, data point, or example
- Closing = signal line or CTA that ties back to the luận điểm

Bullet format options by language:
- Vietnamese: emoji bullets (👉, 🔹, ✅, 🛠, 📈) + short label + explanation
- English: plain bullet (•) or checkmark (✅, ✨) + bold label + 1-2 sentence explanation

## VOICE & TONE

**Core character:**
- Practitioner, not pundit. Always writes from lived experience: client work, real campaigns, partner events. No generic hot takes.
- Quiet confidence. Shares observations, not prescriptions. Rarely says "you should" - says "what we found" or "what stuck with me."
- Warm but not effusive. Grateful without being sycophantic. Recognition feels earned, not performative.
- Bilingual fluency. Vietnamese posts are casual and local with native idiom. English posts are crisp and globally readable with subtle Vietnam/SEA context.

**Tone by post type:**
- Personal reflection / milestone: Understated, thoughtful, slightly philosophical
- Event recap: Warm + energetic, community-focused
- Case study / CRM insight: Direct, data-backed, practitioner voice
- Partnership announcement: Celebratory but grounded, outcome-focused
- Thought leadership: Observation -> implication, never lecture
- Field notes / survival tips: Candid, earned, slightly self-deprecating. Practitioner talking to peers, not advising juniors.
- Workshop recap + field insight: Energetic event context + practitioner insight extracted from doing the work. Emoji-rich bullets, identity close.
- Milestone reflection / anniversary: Casual, self-aware, internally-directed. States personal principles and conditions for staying. Not celebratory - more like a performance review Haji runs on themselves.

## STRUCTURE PATTERNS

### English Posts: "Reframe -> Insight -> Implication" Arc

1. Hook: A reframe or counterintuitive observation (1-2 lines, no preamble). States the luận điểm clearly.
2. Context: What event, client, or moment this comes from (1-2 sentences max)
3. Insight bullets: 3-5 bullets, each with a bold label + concrete point. This is the body.
4. Personal pivot: "For me personally..." or "After X years..." - a quiet reflection
5. Signal line: One closing observation about market direction or behavior shift
6. Optional CTA: A question to the community, only when genuine

Hallmarks:
- Arrow notation: ideas -> live -> iterate (shows causality/sequence, use "->" not "->")
- Short punchy sentences after longer ones
- Colon ":" to introduce contrasts or elaborations, never em dash
- Avoids exclamation marks in analytical sections; uses them sparingly in celebration posts

### Vietnamese Posts: "Myth-Bust -> Real Story -> Lesson" Arc

1. Hook sentence: A reframe of a common assumption. States the luận điểm. ("Subscriber không 'chết'...")
2. Local market context: Acknowledges Vietnam-specific dynamics (mobile-first, OTT dominance, etc.)
3. The real problem: Diagnosis section, specific and data-backed (1-2 short paragraphs)
4. Solution bullets: 3-5 emoji bullets organizing the approach (🛠, 🧨, 📈, 👉, 🔹)
5. Result: Concrete metric with ✅ markers
6. Closing insight: One reflective sentence tying back to luận điểm
7. CTA: Friendly community question ("Bạn đang làm gì?")
8. FYI note (optional): Scope disclaimer at the end if needed

Hallmarks:
- Mixes Vietnamese with English technical terms naturally: "Web Push", "opt-in", "bulk push", "retention", "CRM", "AOV"
- Uses "=>" or "->" for transitions/causality in prose
- Conversational asides: "Nhưng nếu vận hành đúng...", "Lý do không nằm ở..."
- Hashtags grouped at end, 5-8 max, mix of English and Vietnamese-market relevant

### Field Notes / Survival Tips Posts (English): "Experience -> Numbered Lessons -> Human Close" Arc

This is the most personal and transferable post type. Used for practitioner-to-practitioner knowledge sharing, drawn from years in the field.

1. Framing line: States the scope and credibility anchor. "After X years doing Y across Z, here are things I learned the hard way." NOT "here is my advice." Sets tone as peer, not expert.
2. Source attribution (optional): One sentence crediting mentors or context. "The kind I was lucky enough to learn from my leaders."
3. Numbered lessons: 3-6 items. Each lesson follows this micro-structure:
   - Lesson title (bold, conversational, slightly provocative): "Don't just translate features. Translate intentions."
   - Setup: 1-3 lines of context or common misconception
   - The real insight: what actually happens vs. what people assume
   - CS tip / practical takeaway: labeled clearly, 1-3 lines. Starts with "CS tip:" or equivalent.
4. Final note: A broader philosophical observation that zooms out from the numbered tips. More personal, more vulnerable.
5. Closing line: One sentence that reframes the whole post into a human truth. Often uses "=>" to show the conclusion.

Hallmarks of this arc:
- Numbered structure (1. 2. 3.) not bullets. Numbers signal distinct, hard-won lessons.
- Each lesson has a named tip format: "CS tip:", "The trick:", "What works:"
- Reframe technique inside each lesson: state what people say -> reveal what they actually mean
  - Pattern: "They say [X]. But what they mean is [Y]."
  - Pattern: "Change the question from [X] to [Y]. Then [outcome]."
- Dialogue fragments used to illustrate real moments: "When you ask X - silence."
- The word "chaos" appears comfortably. Reality is messy and Haji names it directly.
- Final close is the most personal paragraph. Drops the framework entirely.
- Closing "=>" used to show the earned conclusion from all the lessons.

What this post type is NOT:
- Not a listicle. The numbered items are stories, not hacks.
- Not prescriptive. "CS tip" labels are invitations, not rules.
- Not theoretical. Every point has a real situation behind it.

### Event/Announcement Posts (English)

- Opens with a bold question or celebratory statement (= luận điểm: why this event matters)
- Lists partners/names with full company tags where possible
- 3-5 highlight bullets with ✅ or ✨ + bold label + outcome
- Ends with forward-looking "here's to..." or "thank you to..."
- Hashtag block: 8-12, relevant to industry and topic

## CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

**What Haji writes about:**
- Real outcomes: Metrics, conversion results, unsub rates. Always grounded.
- System thinking: How platforms, teams, and processes fit together
- Vietnam market nuance: Local consumer behavior, adoption pace, feedback culture
- Partnership value: Co-built results, not vendor stories
- CSM/CRM craft: The practitioner's perspective on retention, activation, lifecycle
- Developing market dynamics: Org chaos, unclear ownership, cross-functional politics in SEA/Vietnam enterprise
- CS philosophy: The human side of customer success - supporting people, not just accounts
- Execution vs. strategy gap: The delta between "having the plan" and "building the operator mindset to execute it"
- Personal identity and values: Self-knowledge, numerology, personal philosophy - woven in naturally, never performed

**What Haji avoids:**
- Hype language: "game-changer", "revolutionary", "disrupting"
- Vague praise: "amazing team", "incredible journey" without substance
- Lecture tone: "Here's what you need to do..."
- Long-form preambles before the main point
- The word "straightforward" or "honestly" (sounds AI-generated)
- Em dash " - " and en dash "–" in prose

## SIGNATURE PHRASES & PATTERNS

**English:**
- "X doesn't come from having more Y. It comes from..." (reframe, use period not dash)
- "A small but meaningful thing..." (quiet appreciation)
- "The best signal? ..." (market observation)
- "Not just [X]. But [what actually matters]." (dual contrast, separate sentences)
- "In a space crowded with [trend], staying grounded in [reality]..." (closing observation)
- Arrow flow: [input] -> [action] -> [outcome]
- "These aren't [X]. They're [more honest description]." (field notes opener)
- "They say [X]. But what they mean is [Y]." (client intent reframe)
- "Change the question to [X]. Then [insight]." (reframe via better question)
- "Your job is not to [surface task]. It's to [real task]." (role redefinition)
- "[Concept] sounds [adjective]. But when you ask [honest question] - silence." (reality check pattern)
- "You don't just support [thing]. You support [human reality]." (humanizing close)
- "Everyone talks about [X]. But the reality? [gap between talk and execution]." (workshop insight opener)
- "Simplify, simplify, simplify." (appears explicitly when discussing enablement or complexity)
- "Stop chasing [external thing]. Just keep expanding what you're naturally good at." (milestone reflection, self-knowledge framing)
- "As long as [specific condition holds] - I'll still be here." (integrity close for milestone posts, signals staying by choice not inertia)
- "Many companies have [X], but finding a whole team of both [X] and [Y]? That's rare." (values differentiation pattern)

**Vietnamese:**
- "[Noun] không [verb]. Họ chỉ đang..." (reframe hook, use period not dash)
- "Lý do không nằm ở [obvious thing]. Mà..." (diagnosis redirect)
- "Nếu vận hành đúng, [X] vẫn là..." (conditional insight)
- "Giá trị của [X] không nằm ở [surface]. Mà ở [depth]." (closing philosophy)

## FORMATTING RULES

- Line breaks: Generous. Each idea gets its own line or short paragraph.
- Bullets: 3-6 per post, each 1-3 sentences. Emoji in Vietnamese, plain or ✅/✨ in English.
- Bold: For bullet labels and key phrases only. Not overused.
- No em dash " - " anywhere. Substitute with: colon, period, or line break.
- Length targets:
  - Personal reflection / thought leadership: 200-400 words
  - Case study / CRM insight: 350-550 words
  - Event recap: 200-350 words
  - Partnership announcement: 150-250 words
  - Field notes / survival tips: 400-650 words (each lesson needs room to breathe)
  - Workshop recap + field insight: 250-400 words
  - Milestone reflection / anniversary: 200-350 words (tight - impact comes from compression)
- Hashtags: Always at end. Vietnamese: mix local (#martech, #CRM, #retention). English: broader (#CustomerExperience, #AI, #CDP).

## WORKFLOW FOR DRAFTING

1. Identify post type: case study / event / announcement / reflection / insight / field notes / workshop recap / milestone
2. Identify language: Vietnamese / English / bilingual
3. Define the luận điểm first: one sentence that is the central argument of this post
4. Apply matching arc from structure patterns above
5. Draft hook: states the luận điểm, stops the scroll
6. Write supporting content:
   - Most posts: 3-6 emoji or plain bullets, each = one concrete idea, data point, or example
   - Field notes: numbered lessons (3-6), each with setup + real insight + CS tip label
   - Workshop recap: 4-5 emoji insight bullets (💡🧩🌾👀) + identity close paragraph
   - Milestone: ✅ personal principles block + 💡🚀❤️ reasons block + conditional close
7. Close with signal line or CTA tied back to luận điểm
8. Final check: scan for " - " and replace every instance. Post fails if any em dash remains.
9. Add hashtag block

### Workshop Recap + Field Insight Posts (English): "Action -> Observation -> Identity" Arc

Used after hands-on partner workshops, training sessions, or field visits. Combines event recap with practitioner insight extracted from the experience.

1. Title/hook: Bold claim about what was proven or observed, with context (location, partners, team). Names collaborators with @tags. Emoji used in title to signal energy.
2. Transition: "While wrapping up, I had a moment of reflection:" or similar. Signals pivot from event recap to personal insight.
3. Insight bullets: 4-5 emoji bullets (💡🧩🌾👀), each carrying ONE practitioner observation. Format: emoji + bold reframe label + 2-3 sentences of explanation. Each bullet contradicts a common assumption.
4. Identity close: A paragraph anchoring the observations to who Haji is and what they stand for. References years of experience, a personal mission or phrase ("Empowering Local"), and a forward-looking statement.
5. Optional hiring CTA: Brief, warm, values-led. "If you're looking for a place where you can make an impact... Insider might just be the right place." Followed by a link. Never pushy.

Hallmarks:
- Emoji variety in body bullets: 💡 for insight, 🧩 for systems/complexity, 🌾 for simplicity/growth, 👀 for observation/reality check
- The "simplify, simplify, simplify" principle often appears explicitly or implicitly
- Tension between "what people claim" vs "what execution actually requires" is the recurring theme
- Identity statement at end is earned, not performed: ties back to specific years, specific markets, specific mission

### Milestone Reflection / Anniversary Posts (English): "Checkpoint -> Principles -> Conditions" Arc

Used for work anniversaries, year-end reflections, or personal milestones. More personal and internally-directed than other post types. Less about external insight, more about self-accounting.

1. Opening: A casual, specific trigger ("checked my Slack and realized..."). Sets a grounded, unheroic tone. Uses emoji sparingly.
2. Personal principles block: 2-3 ✅ bullets. These are personal rules or mindset shifts - stated directly, often referencing self-knowledge. Can include personal frameworks, numerology (Haji references "Number 7" - their life path number influencing how they work), or identity markers.
3. Reasons to stay block: 3 bullets with distinct emojis (💡🚀❤️) for 3 different values. Each bullet = one concrete thing that makes the company worth staying for. Specific, not generic.
4. Conditional close: NOT "I love this place." Instead: "As long as [specific condition holds], I'll stay." Shows integrity and self-respect. The condition is about values alignment, not comfort.

Hallmarks:
- No preamble, no throat-clearing. Opens mid-thought.
- Self-awareness is explicit: "I can't convince myself to trust a plan unless I've actually done it."
- References personal identity markers naturally: numerology, archetypes, personal philosophy (Entrepreneur mindset, Number 7)
- The closing condition signals someone who stays by choice, not inertia
- Warmth without sentimentality: "Many companies have smarter people, but finding a whole team of both smart & kind people? That's rare."
- Hashtags are minimal and personal for this type: #CustomerSuccess #lifeatInsider + 2-3 topic tags
